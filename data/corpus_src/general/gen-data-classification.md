# Data classification policy

Velmora Systems classifies information so that everyone handles it with the right level of care. Every document, dataset, and message belongs to a classification level. The levels, from least to most sensitive, are {{fact:dataclass.levels}}.

## Public

Information approved for anyone to see, such as published marketing pages, press releases, and open job postings. Public information can be shared freely, but only the communications team can make something public.

## Internal

Everyday working information that is not meant for outsiders: project plans, meeting notes, internal announcements, and most email. Internal information may be shared with employees and with contractors who need it. Share it externally only under a signed agreement and with the data owner's approval.

## Confidential

Information that could harm Velmora, a customer, or an employee if it leaked. Examples include customer contracts, unreleased product plans, financial results before they are announced, and source code. Confidential information:

- may be stored only on company systems;
- must be shared with named people, never through public links;
- must be labeled "Confidential" in the document header.

## Restricted

The most sensitive information: credentials and keys, personal data such as compensation and health information, security incident details, and customer data covered by contracts. Restricted information:

- is stored only in systems approved for it; credentials and keys belong in {{fact:dataclass.restricted_storage}};
- is never sent by email attachment or chat;
- is accessed only by people whose role requires it, and that access is logged;
- is never pasted into unapproved tools, including AI assistants.

## Labeling

Label documents in the header or the first line. When a document mixes levels, use the highest level that applies. If you are unsure, treat information as Confidential and ask the data owner.

## Data owners

Every system and shared drive has a data owner, usually the leader of the team that created the data. Owners decide who may access their data and approve external sharing.

## Mistakes

If you send or store information at the wrong level, for example by emailing a restricted spreadsheet to the wrong person, report it to the security team right away. Quick reporting limits harm, and good-faith mistakes are treated as learning opportunities.
