# Wi-Fi and guest network

Every Velmora office has a wireless network for company devices and a separate network for visitors. Using the right one keeps internal systems safe and keeps guests productive.

## Company network

The company network is called VEL-Corp. Company laptops and enrolled phones join it automatically using a device certificate, so you never type a password. If your laptop does not connect:

1. Make sure Wi-Fi is turned on and airplane mode is off.
2. Forget the network and let the laptop rejoin it.
3. Restart the laptop.
4. If it still fails, the device certificate may have expired; open a Helpdesk ticket.

Personal devices cannot join VEL-Corp, even with a colleague's help. Do not try to copy certificates between devices.

## Guest network

Visitors, interviewees, and vendors use VEL-Guest. Guest access is separate from company systems; guests can browse the internet but cannot reach internal sites, printers, or file shares.

Guest passes are issued by the reception desk. Each pass is valid for 24 hours. For visitors staying longer, such as auditors or contractors on a multi-day engagement, the host should ask reception to issue a new pass each day, or request a sponsored contractor account through the Helpdesk.

Hosts are responsible for their guests. Please do not share your own credentials with a visitor, and do not connect a visitor's device to a meeting room's wired port.

## Your personal devices

You may use VEL-Guest for personal phones and tablets during breaks. Personal devices that need email should be enrolled as described on the mobile devices and BYOD page; once enrolled, they can use the company network.

## Coverage problems

If a part of the office has weak coverage, report the location, such as the room name or the desk label on the floor plan, through a Helpdesk ticket. The network team collects these reports and adjusts access points during scheduled maintenance.

## Security notes

- Never set up your own wireless access point or hotspot inside an office. Unauthorized access points are detected and disabled, and the owner is contacted.
- Be careful with public networks in cafes and airports. Use the VPN for internal work, and avoid signing in to sensitive sites from shared computers.
- If you see a network with a name that imitates ours, report it to the security team.

## Events

For large events with many outside visitors, Workplace Services can arrange an event network. Ask well in advance so the network team can plan capacity, and share the expected number of guests and the rooms involved.
