# Backups for end users

Company laptops are backed up automatically, so a lost, stolen, or failed laptop does not mean lost work. This page explains what is backed up, how to restore files yourself, and what to do when a restore shows a warning.

## What is backed up

The backup agent copies your documents, desktop, and downloads folders every 4 hours while your laptop is online. Files on shared drives are protected separately and do not need laptop backups. Backups are encrypted on your laptop before they are sent.

Previous versions of your files are kept for 180 days. After that, only the most recent version of each file is kept.

## What is not backed up

- Applications and system files; these are reinstalled from the Software Catalog.
- Files in folders you excluded yourself.
- Very large media files; keep those on a team drive or the media archive.
- Personal files, which should not be on a company laptop.

## Restoring files yourself

Use VelVault Restore, which is installed on every company laptop:

1. Open VelVault Restore from the start menu.
2. Browse to the folder that held the file.
3. Choose the date you want to restore from.
4. Select the files and choose Restore. Restored files appear in a new folder named after the restore date, so nothing you have now is overwritten.

## Warnings during a restore

Restores sometimes show the message "Some files could not be verified." This usually means the laptop went to sleep during a recent backup. Ignore the warning and run the restore again; the next attempt uses an earlier verified copy. If the warning keeps appearing, stop and open a Helpdesk ticket, because the backup may be damaged.

If the tool says "Restore blocked by policy," the file is classified as restricted and must be restored by the Helpdesk.

## Restoring to a new laptop

When you receive a replacement laptop, the Helpdesk restores your files during the swap appointment. You can also restore them yourself afterward with VelVault Restore.

## Checking that backups work

Open VelVault Restore and look at the status bar, which shows the time of the last successful backup. If it looks out of date while you have been working online, open a Helpdesk ticket.

## Privacy

Backups are encrypted, and only the Helpdesk's restore service can read them. Helpdesk staff open your files only when you request a restore and give permission.
